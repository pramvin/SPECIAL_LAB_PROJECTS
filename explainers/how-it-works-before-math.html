How was news sentiment trained?

English Benzinga headlines received training labels from the Hu & Liu and Loughran–McDonald word lists. Those labels then trained a TF-IDF (single words, English stop words removed) + LinearSVC classifier. This is learning to reproduce word-list labels, not learning from human judgments or market reactions. Only the headline is scored, not the full article.

Reported 94.5% agreement on the 2018 check set is agreement with those generated labels, not human-verified accuracy. Softmax over SVM margins is not calibrated probability. This page does not run that classifier; its examples are explanatory scenarios.

What exactly counts as a “big move”?

At decision day t, the training target uses the return from the close at t+1 to the close at t+6: five trading days. It subtracts that day's cross-stock median forward return, takes the absolute difference, and labels values above the 80th percentile as big moves. A large positive or negative deviation can qualify. It is not a fixed price-change threshold or an intraday range.

The 10 chart features are centered against each day's cross-stock median. HistGradientBoostingClassifier learns the target, then Isotonic Regression calibrates the probability. Training uses pre-2022 data, calibration uses 2022, and the reported test period is 2023–October 2026. Reported AUC: 0.674 versus 0.663 for 20-day volatility alone. AUC is not percent accuracy; the improvement over this simple baseline is small. Historical evaluation does not establish profitability.
